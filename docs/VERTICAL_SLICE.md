# The Ashen Standard

## Premise

Gascony, 1356. At the fictional Battle of Saint-Orens Ford, a fractured
Anglo-Gascon vanguard loses its standard while an opposing spear formation
closes on the bridge. The player is a landless man-at-arms whose local actions
can turn a collapse into an organized counterattack.

The world should be materially plausible for the Hundred Years' War without
using real historical celebrities or copied fictional settings.

## Player fantasy

The player is not a supernatural army-killer. They are the decisive fighter
whose aggression, rescues, officer pressure, and battlefield commands convert
nearby panic into coordinated violence.

## Target run

A complete playthrough should last approximately 5–10 minutes:

1. Leave the baggage line and see the battlefield reveal.
2. Fight through a collapsing melee and recover the fallen standard.
3. Rally allies at a hedgerow and withstand a counterattack.
4. Break the enemy spear line directly or through the mill flank.
5. Advance to the ford and defeat the enemy captain.
6. Raise the captured standard and secure the bridge.

## Signature systems

### Formation cohesion

Local squads transition visibly through:

`ordered -> pressured -> fractured -> routed`

Casualties, flanking, officer loss, missile pressure, standards, and player
orders affect cohesion. The principal win condition is breaking collective
resistance, not personally depleting hundreds of health bars.

### Retinue presence

Nearby allies may recognize the player as a temporary battlefield leader.
Contextual commands should include rally, brace, advance, and focus where
scope permits.

### Grounded weapon contact

Combat prioritizes reach, timing, guard, shove, stamina, armor response, and
strong audiovisual contact. Damage numbers should not replace readable
animation, sound, posture, and material response.

### Battlefield reversal

Objectives must visibly alter the battle. Rallying allies, killing officers,
breaking the spear line, and taking the ford should move formations and change
the state of the world rather than merely updating UI text.

## Battlefield composition

Use one compact but layered battlefield:

- baggage embankment and opening reveal;
- muddy central melee lane;
- hedgerow rally pocket;
- burning mill and optional flank;
- spear-line approach;
- stone ford or bridge gate;
- distant battle belts for cavalry, smoke, banners, arrows, and troop scale.

Communicate hundreds of combatants with simulation tiers rather than attempting
hundreds of full-detail brains. Near agents receive full behavior, mid-field
agents receive reduced updates, and far ranks are honest real-time instanced
visual simulation.

## Visual direction

- Cold rain or post-storm slate light.
- Churned umber mud, wet stone, trampled grain, standing water.
- Dark iron, scratched steel, tarnished brass.
- Madder red, woad blue, faded ochre, and undyed wool for faction readability.
- Restrained orange fire near the mill.
- Strong near-, mid-, and far-field composition from first-person eye height.
- Controlled impact motion rather than constant camera shake.

## Scope discipline

Do not expand this slice into an open world, campaign economy, crafting game,
multiplayer project, mounted-player system, or multi-weapon content catalogue
before the core battlefield loop is convincing. Density, choreography,
responsiveness, consequence, and a transformed battlefield matter more than
map size.

## Slice-specific acceptance

- A player can complete an opening-to-victory flow without developer tools.
- Combat allows attacking, defending, stamina use, damage, and death.
- Multiple simultaneous squads demonstrate factional combat and morale states.
- Player actions change formation behavior and battlefield ownership.
- The environment blends active near combat with convincing distant scale.
- Victory leaves the battlefield visibly different from its opening state.
- The captain follows shared combat rules rather than functioning only as a
  disconnected scripted health bar.
- No milestone is satisfied solely through a cinematic or static visual effect.
